# COP Memory — Necessity and Local Equilibrium

## 1. Object

This note records a bridge between situated judgment and systems theory.

The ordinary formula is:

```text
nécessité fait loi
necessity makes law
```

This does not mean that necessity abolishes judgment. It means that the local configuration of constraints can become so strong that it defines what action remains possible, reasonable or survivable.

In COP terms:

```text
behavior_trace + circumstances -> situated interpretation -> situated judgment
```

The same behavior may be judged differently when the local constraints change.

## 2. Local necessity

A necessity is not an abstract moral property. It is a situated pressure field.

It may include:

```text
threat
scarcity
urgency
power asymmetry
available alternatives
energy cost
information deficit
legal constraint
social pressure
ecological constraint
survival constraint
```

A judgment that ignores these constraints risks becoming a context-free moral illusion.

## 3. Local equilibrium analogy

In physical and systems language, the closest analogy is not necessarily global equilibrium.

It is closer to:

```text
local equilibrium
local optimum
attractor
basin of attraction
metastable state
nonequilibrium steady state
homeostatic regime
```

A system may settle into a locally stable configuration that is not globally optimal.

This matters for judgment:

```text
what is locally necessary may not be globally desirable
what is locally stable may not be morally ideal
what is locally rational may remain tragically costly
```

## 4. Attractors and circumstances

A situation can be read as a basin of constraints.

Within that basin, certain behaviors become more likely, more stable, cheaper, or more necessary than others.

COP should therefore distinguish:

```text
behavior_trace      = what happened or was observed
constraint_field    = local pressures shaping possible action
local_equilibrium   = relatively stable response under those constraints
interpretation      = current reading of that response
judgment            = evaluative conclusion under explicit criteria
escape_condition    = what would allow the system to leave the local basin
```

The escape condition is important. If one wants to change behavior, one often has to change the basin of constraints rather than merely condemn the observed action.

## 5. Necessity is not absolution

The phrase "necessity makes law" must not become a universal excuse.

COP should mark necessity claims as claims:

```text
necessity_claim = asserted explanation that a constraint field made an action locally necessary or strongly favored
```

Such a claim should expose:

```text
claimed constraint
available alternatives
costs of alternatives
risk level
who asserts necessity
who contests it
what evidence supports it
what evidence could falsify it
```

Necessity can explain. It does not automatically justify.

## 6. Practical invariant

```text
Never judge a behavior without modelling the local constraint field.
Never let a claim of necessity silently pass as moral absolution.
Never confuse local equilibrium with global justice.
```

## 7. Stable formula

```text
Circumstances define the local constraint field.
Necessity is the pressure exerted by that field.
Behavior often settles into local equilibria.
Judgment must distinguish explanation, justification and transformation.
```

For COP/Memory:

```text
A situated judgment should not merely classify an act.
It should expose the local constraints, the local equilibrium, the alternatives, and the conditions under which the judgment could change.
```
